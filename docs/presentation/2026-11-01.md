# Halftime, break animation, frame cadence, and team identity

## Reproduction

Installed 1.0.29: Scores showed Browns 6, Steelers 20, Halftime. Opening event 401873027 showed 1:10 in the first quarter while the football stage labeled itself Halftime. A captured ESPN summary reproduces the exact 1:10 first-quarter timeout in an offline regression before the fix.

The timeline bounded every play at a stopped game clock as if its timestamp belonged to the preceding stoppage. A reception following the Q1 timeout had its own valid wallclock. The next clock-changing play had an estimated timestamp; the restart search rejected it. The cluster's upper bound consequently became the page's arrival time and delayed the rest of the game.

## Changes

- Honor independently stamped stopped-clock plays. Retain bounded scheduling for reused stoppage timestamps and accept an interpolated restart estimate.
- Observe the opening scoreboard, remove the ticking clock and possession during an interval, and label break states consistently on the page.
- Timeout and official-timeout scenes travel to the sidelines once and settle until a new play arrives. Preserve stoppage metadata so the directed stage distinguishes a two-minute warning, halftime, and final.
- Show confirmed official timeouts before stale down-and-distance in score tiles. Do not claim to know the TV commercial schedule.
- Visible actor travel and camera movement receive 60 Hz scheduling; settled scenes remain idle. Cache football clip loop bounds and prepare uniforms outside the per-frame closure.
- Preserve valid provider primary colors; use extracted logo colors only when source colors are missing. Hydrate historical match colors from summary team metadata by ID. Background washes retain each team's identity even when opponents share a color.
- Keep black uniform colors black. Use a supplied alternate when teams need more contrast; when no alternate works, give the away team an illustrative contrast kit, accounting for soccer's home-first ordering. Label the drawn colors with Home/Away. The legend describes team colors, not verified match-day kits.

## Evidence and limits

ESPN Liverpool team 364 reports primary `d11317` and alternate `FFFFFF`; the sampled team and match feeds did not provide a match-specific jersey assignment. No real-match uniform claims are made.

The initial regression reported 2 passed / 2 failed and reproduced `1:10 - 1st`, last play `401873027955`. The first fixed run passed the halftime, prolonged-break, color identity, and walking-cadence regressions. Its still-scene test double incorrectly advertised continuous motion; that test double was corrected before the final run.

The final standard and unrestricted native runs each passed **3,035 checks, zero failures**, including all 25 focused presentation regressions. DEBUG application typechecking passed. Scoreline **1.0.30 (32)** is installed and verified; release evidence is retained below. This work does not close the later choreography grammar, renderer-allocation, whole-game CPU, or web timing-engine phases described in `docs/synchronization/STATUS.md`.

## Native pixel-check correction

The first unrestricted full run reported 3,034 passes and one failure: the existing fade comparison saw a maximum 4/255 RGB difference at partially covered edges. The same executable passed the check in the restricted environment. The figure renderer is byte-for-byte unchanged from 1.0.29 (`TheaterKit.swift` SHA-256 `d01d4bfd1513ce7d501ec1b8c659e90338dc42681815d68357392d01cf08afa3`).

Inspection of all five rendered poses found correct alpha everywhere and correct colors in fully covered pixels. The check now retains its 2/255 tolerance for alpha across every pixel and for RGB in fully covered pixels; it also requires nonempty interior coverage. RGB edge resampling remains reported separately. This tests the actual opacity/overlap contract without changing the application's renderer or raising those tolerances. The failing receipt is retained beside the final run.
