## Current behavior

Soccer, football, basketball and baseball now share the compact timeline design and the same wall-clock domain as their probability chart, including existing college competitions. The event rail is 60 points tall, team labels sit on the left, and event details replace the period-label row. Football keeps one scoring rail; the other sports place attributed events above/below the center rule. The timeline has no separate box, shaded period bands or inspector footer.

Timed soccer now uses the shared OddsPointer. Hover, pin, previous/next event, Escape and Live/Latest operate across the two modules. The isolated MatchFocusBridge preserves existing play-ID highlighting with soccer Key Events and the pitch. It does not fabricate an event for a nearby market timestamp, overwrite an unrelated row pin, or resurrect an old selection after clearing. Both modules schedule reveals from actual event/period timestamps as well as market ticks, so a quiet market does not delay a known play.

Provider limits remain explicit: minute-only soccer events retain the original SoccerTimeline fallback with an unavailable-timestamps notice. Partial untimed plays remain in Key Events. The app does not invent kickoff-plus-minute timestamps or claim the fallback aligns with wall-clock odds. CS2/climbing retain their distinct round/attempt views. Inspection still does not rewind live playback; replay actions remain separate.

## Original soccer timeline restoration (installed and visually verified)

The user explicitly rejected changing the soccer module that served as the design reference. `SoccerTimeline` in `Visualizations.swift` is byte-for-byte unchanged from release `5509755` (1.0.30) and the original Developer checkout. The regression was the newer `DetailView` route: whenever soccer had absolute event history, it selected `MatchTimelineSection` instead.

Soccer now always uses the original component, with its original live-empty visibility and captions. This restores one central rule, neutral team abbreviations, goal/card marks above and below, the 0′/HT/90′ landmarks and responsive quarter-hour ticks, the live progress fill/red dot, and the existing LinkedFocus interaction with Key Events and the pitch. Hover detail replaces the minute labels within 60 points; no boxed well, period bands, second rule, or separate inspector is added. Key Event replay actions and their provider IDs are untouched. Other sports keep the current timeline implementation, including football's single compact rail.

The intentional limit is axis semantics: the restored soccer rail measures match minutes, while Polymarket measures wall time. Halftime and stoppages make those scales different, so equal horizontal positions do not promise equal moments. Soccer odds still offer factual timestamp inspection and reported event context. The restoration does not invent an alignment or alter the trusted soccer component to obtain one. A future linked-selection bridge would require its own design and validation.

A compiler-free CPU capture of the unpublished `75bb09a` installation found a sustained SwiftUI update loop; see [the diagnosis](evidence/hover-loop-diagnosis.json). The selected accessibility value had accidentally subscribed the odds hover input node to the state it changes, causing repeated hover-end/active callbacks. The correction isolates the observed accessibility read. The app was stopped after capturing the trace; the last installed binary is preserved for comparison. This specific loop is distinct from the user's earlier result-frame stutter report and must not be presented as proof that every animation issue is resolved.

A separate deterministic football bug applied `ClipWarp` twice to a free ball while players used it once. The painter now uses the clip-time sample directly.
